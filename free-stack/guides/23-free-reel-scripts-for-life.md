# 23. Free Reel Scripts for Life

## Step 1. Learn the five beats

Every script in this system hits five beats, in this order, timed for a 30 to 45 second reel. Longer format, stretch the middle two beats, never the hook or the CTA.

| Beat | Time budget | Job |
|---|---|---|
| Hook | 0 to 3 seconds | Stop the scroll. Nothing else matters if this fails. |
| Context | 3 to 8 seconds | One sentence, why anyone should care what comes next. |
| Proof | 8 to 25 seconds | The actual content. A number, a demonstration, a receipt. |
| Payoff | 25 to 35 seconds | What that proof means for the viewer, specifically. |
| CTA | 35 to 40 seconds | One action, said once, clearly. |

> **Why the hook gets a hard 3-second budget and nothing else does.** Every short-form platform's own data says the same thing: the decision to keep watching happens almost immediately. A script that spends its first five seconds on a slow windup has already lost most of its audience before the actual point arrives.

## Step 2. Pick a hook archetype

Five that reliably work. Fill in the brackets, do not overthink the exact wording on the first pass.

**The Cost Reveal**
"I just found out [surprising cost or savings fact]."
Use when: you have a real number that will surprise people.

**The Myth Bust**
"Everyone thinks [common belief]. Here's why that's wrong."
Use when: you're correcting a widespread, specific misconception in your field.

**The Receipt Drop**
"Here's exactly what [thing] actually costs, no guessing."
Use when: you have documentation, a screenshot, a real invoice, something to show, not just say.

**The Countdown**
"[Number] things [your audience] doesn't know about [topic]."
Use when: you have three or more genuinely distinct points, not one point said three ways.

**The Before and After**
"This is what [thing] looked like before, and after."
Use when: you have a visual, or describable, transformation to show side by side.

Pick whichever one matches the content you actually have, not whichever one sounds best in the abstract. A Receipt Drop hook with no receipt to show is a broken promise five seconds in.

## Step 3. Fill in the full template

Copy this for every new script.

```
TOPIC: 
HOOK ARCHETYPE: 

[0-3s] HOOK
"..."

[3-8s] CONTEXT
One sentence. Why does this matter, right now, to this specific viewer?

[8-25s] PROOF
The actual substance. A number, a demo, a document, a comparison.
Write it as you would say it out loud, not as you would write it.

[25-35s] PAYOFF
So what? Translate the proof into what it means for them, specifically,
not what it means in general.

[35-40s] CTA
One instruction. "Comment X." "Link in bio." Not two, not three, one.
```

## Step 4. Read it out loud before you film

Time yourself. If any single beat runs long, cut from the Proof section first, that is almost always where scripts bloat, never from the Hook or the CTA.

---

## Verify it works

- [ ] The hook, read alone with no context, would make you personally stop scrolling
- [ ] The Context beat is one sentence, not two
- [ ] The Proof beat has one real, specific thing in it, a number or a document, not a vague claim
- [ ] The Payoff explicitly connects back to the viewer, not just to the topic in general
- [ ] The CTA gives exactly one instruction
- [ ] Read out loud at a natural pace, the whole thing lands under 40 seconds

---

## What breaks and how to fix it

**The hook feels weak even after picking an archetype**
The archetype is a shape, not a guarantee. If "I just found out X" is not actually surprising to your specific audience, the Cost Reveal is the wrong archetype for this topic, try the Myth Bust instead.

**The script runs long no matter what you cut**
Almost always the Proof beat trying to cover more than one point. Split it, this is two scripts, not one over-stuffed one.

**The CTA gets ignored**
Usually because it competes with a second, unstated CTA, "follow for more" tacked onto the actual instruction. State one action. If you want both a follow and a comment, that is worth split-testing as two different videos, not asking for both in one breath.

**Every script starts to sound the same after a while**
Correct, on purpose, up to a point. The structure repeating is what makes it recognizable as yours. Rotate which of the five hook archetypes you reach for so the shape stays familiar while the opening line does not always sound identical.

**You cannot tell if a draft is actually good before filming it**
Read it to one other person, out loud, and watch their face at the three-second mark, not their reaction to the whole thing. That is the only beat with zero room for a slow start.
